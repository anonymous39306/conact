§ 0 · Abstract

The missing half of a demonstration.

Human videos show where the hands move, but not how firmly they press, and this missing contact is why policies pretrained on them still need many teleoperated demonstrations.

ConAct (Contact as Action) extends the point-based action shared by human and robot hands with a contact intensity at each 3D keypoint, labeled automatically on about 1M egocentric episodes. The policy learns from human videos how firmly to press, and at deployment the predicted contact becomes force along the normals of the robot hand, without any force sensing.

With only 20 teleoperated demonstrations in total, ConAct more than doubles the average success rate of the strongest VLA (17.2% → 41.7%) and already matches a baseline trained with ten times more. On a bimanual task, it more than quadruples the best baseline with only five teleoperated demonstrations (12.5% → 53.1%).

§ II · Method

Points in, keypoints and contact out.

Everything lives in the metric 3D space of the ego camera, so a trajectory demonstrated by a human hand is directly a target for the robot hand.

Step 1 · label

Contact from ordinary video

A video hand tracker reconstructs each hand's mesh with a contact intensity per vertex; we pool it to the 21 MANO keypoints. No tactile glove, no manual annotation — for about 1M egocentric episodes and our own demos alike.

ct ∈ [0,1]21 per hand
Step 2 · learn

One action for both hands

A diffusion transformer reads object points, both hands' keypoints and the instruction, and predicts the next 16 keypoint positions with a contact intensity at each — in pretraining and fine-tuning alike.

(Ĥ, ĉ)t+1:t+H = πθ(Ht, Ot, ℓ)
Step 3 · deploy

Contact becomes force

At deployment each keypoint target moves along the direction in which its finger closes, in proportion to its contact. Inverse kinematics tracks the displaced targets, so the hand presses into the object.

x̃k = x̂k + δ ĉk nk
𝓛 = 𝓛act + λ 𝓛ct  ·  rectified flow + contact BCE
§ III · Results

Fewer robot demos, same hands.

Every method is fine-tuned as a single policy on the same human demonstrations plus 10%, 50% or 100% of the teleoperated demonstrations of each task, i.e., up to 100 for Pick & Place and 50 per bimanual task, on an RB-Y1 with two 20-DoF Wuji hands. ConAct leads on every task at every share, and its margin grows as the robot data shrinks: +13% relative to VITRA with all of it, +21% with 50% and +142% with 10%. With 10%, it already exceeds GR00T N1.7 trained with all of it on average (41.7% vs. 40.6%).

§ IV · Contact-force injection

Contact into force, without force sensing.

Point targets cannot express how firmly to press. So ConAct realizes contact by displacing the targets of inverse kinematics: each of the 21 keypoints is mapped to a site on the robot hand's kinematic model with a unit normal along the flexion direction of the motor that drives it, and each predicted keypoint moves along its normal in proportion to its predicted contact.

x̃k = x̂k + δ ĉk nk  ·  one δ shared by all keypoints, tasks and objects
  • In contactthe target lies slightly beyond the object surface, so the position-controlled joints press the hand into the object with a force that grows with the intensity.
  • No heuristicsonly the mapped sites and their normals are used, with contact-weighted IK; no hand-specific grip rules.

§ VI · Why contact

Points alone stop at the surface.

Without contact prediction and the contact-driven offset, the policy's action is only a set of points: the fingers reach the ball but close no further than its surface, so the grasp is too loose to lift it. With contact as part of the action, the same keypoints are pushed into the object and the hand holds it. On Pick & Place with 10% of the teleoperation data, removing the force injection drops the success rate from 56.3% to 25.0%.
